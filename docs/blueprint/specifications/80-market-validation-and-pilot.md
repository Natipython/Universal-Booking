# Market validation and controlled pilot

Status: **Draft — no interviews, enrolled merchants or pilot results yet**  
Updated: 2026-10-04 (Africa/Addis_Ababa)  
Depends on: DEC-001–004, release contract 72, support 65, scorecard 83

## Narrow hypothesis

Ethiopia is decided. Candidate focus: Addis Ababa photo studios/photographers that currently combine calls, walk-ins, chats and notebooks. Hypothesis: fast manual capture plus a reliable calendar and simple customer links reduces forgotten/conflicting work enough that merchants keep using and paying for Ubi.

Do not assume all businesses have smartphones, separate reception staff, reliable data, English fluency, official social business accounts or enough daily bookings to justify a subscription. Measure these differences before designing onboarding or pricing.

## Discovery, before coding the entire market

Proposed recruitment: 12–15 businesses for exploratory interviews, with owners, receptionists and photographers represented; 12–20 customer task participants across digital-confidence/language groups. This is qualitative discovery, not a representative Ethiopian survey or a conversion forecast.

Observe, with appropriate permission:

1. How a real enquiry begins, who answers and how a date/price becomes agreed.
2. Where call/walk-in bookings are written, who checks the calendar and what happens when staff disagree.
3. Whether studio sessions, weddings and on-location jobs require different workflows/resources/buffers.
4. What is actually paid, when evidence is trusted and who handles cancellation/refund.
5. Which phone/device/network/language/calendar/clock conventions are used.
6. How often mistakes occur and what they cost in time/trust, without inventing monetary savings.
7. Who can authorize account setup, manage staff, connect official channels and approve monthly costs.
8. Whether direct links are shareable from the merchant's existing habits without inbox integration.

Collect minimized, de-identified research notes. Avoid copying real chats, customer notebooks or phone contacts into a prototype. Recording, participant compensation and retention need a scoped research protocol.

## Value tests

| Hypothesis | Experiment | Disconfirming signal |
|---|---|---|
| Manual entry solves a daily problem | Dummy call/walk-in tasks, then permitted shadow comparison | Merchant entry takes longer than their current method and offers no reliable benefit |
| One schedule becomes trustworthy | Reconcile a permitted daily count against the merchant's authoritative record | Important channels remain uncaptured or staff distrust the status |
| Customer link is comprehensible | Moderated standard/custom tasks with limited-digital-experience customers | Frequent wrong dates, false confirmation belief or need for staff to do every step |
| Official connections are optional value | Compare direct-link/manual path with an eligible connector sandbox | Product loses basic value without complex platform account setup |
| Merchants will pay | Present honest scope and proposed price experiments; record later actual collection | Polite enthusiasm with no sustained usage or willingness to pay |

## Proposed pilot design

Ten businesses over 8–12 weeks is a planning candidate, not an approved size or enrollment claim. Begin with two or three supervised merchants after live gates pass; expand only after the first operational review. Keep recruiting businesses with different staff/device conditions, not only friendly technically confident owners.

| Phase | Work | Progress condition |
|---|---|---|
| Baseline/research | Understand existing booking counts, conflicts, time and support need | Consent/protocol, known problem, defined denominator |
| Synthetic prototype | Test manual entry, request/confirmation, custom quote, dates and failure recovery | Critical comprehension/integrity defects resolved |
| Authorized shadow trial | Real-world parallel comparison; merchant's declared source of truth remains explicit | Legal/privacy/live scope cleared; no fake production availability |
| Controlled cutover | Reconcile existing bookings/blocks; designate one authoritative calendar for agreed scope | Owner agreement, import check, support coverage, rollback plan |
| Steady use | Weekly review of task success, capture, retention, faults and cost | Evidence against agreed scorecard, not download count |
| Exit review | Continue/pivot/pause with export/closure and data handling | Named decision, evidence and merchant communication |

Shadow operation and cutover are not invitations to double-confirm bookings in two calendars. Publication needs imported existing commitments or conservative availability blocks. When uncertain, request-to-confirm is a candidate fallback, not an automatic default for every service.

## Pilot inclusion and honest offer

Recruit merchants with actual bookings, accountable owner authorization, a supported device, willingness to capture all agreed sources, and a practical support contact. Do not require a WhatsApp API account merely to join. Explain unsupported services/features, data handling, tentative offline records, response/support hours and test-versus-live boundaries.

Owner/staff training covers adding a call booking, finding it, handling offline/conflict status, distinguishing requests/payment reports, changing a date and asking for help. Training cannot replace correct first-time UI.

## Weekly review and stop controls

Use scorecard 83 with baseline, sample size and segment. Review booking discrepancies daily during supervised cutover; schedule weekly adoption/commercial reviews. Any confirmed tenant leak, lost accepted work, false confirmed/verified status or prohibited double allocation pauses the affected capability and invokes runbooks. Unsupported provider behavior disables that adapter, not all manual booking.

Rework/pivot signals: merchants do not capture their actual work, low-confidence users repeatedly misunderstand dates/status, support demand exceeds funded capacity, or genuine willingness to pay cannot cover attributable costs. Do not widen verticals to hide failure in the first one.

## Required outputs

De-identified interview/task receipts, fixture corrections, baseline data definitions, approved pilot boundary, signed/authorized merchant onboarding records, imported-booking reconciliation, actual owner/support coverage, weekly scorecards, incident log and explicit exit decision. Every output has an owner and appropriate retention; none exist yet.
