## Selected first-launch jurisdiction

The founder selected **Ethiopia** on 2026-09-05. The Ethiopia row and Ethiopian cross-domain workstreams are now the first-release legal research path. The other country rows remain expansion screening only and do not authorize multi-country launch.

## Direct conclusion

“East Africa” is not one legal jurisdiction. Ubi must launch in one selected country/city/vertical through a defined legal entity and obtain licensed local advice across privacy, business/consumer/electronic transactions, payments, tax/records, communications and sector rules.

The [East African Community currently lists eight Partner States](https://www.eac.int/overview-of-eac): Burundi, Democratic Republic of the Congo, Kenya, Rwanda, Somalia, South Sudan, Uganda and Tanzania. Ethiopia is not an EAC Partner State but is included as a separate likely product-market candidate; the user’s Addis Ababa timezone is context, not a launch decision.

## How to use this matrix

- **Verified primary lead:** an official current source establishes the named framework exists; counsel must still interpret applicability.
- **Research required:** no sufficiently current first-party conclusion was established during this screening.
- **Blocking:** feature/entity/provider cannot launch until answered.
- **Not applicable** can be selected only by qualified review, not assumption.

This matrix must be refreshed immediately before country selection and again before launch. Laws, directives, regulator practice, provider eligibility and tax rules change.

## Payment boundary screening

Ubi’s safe initial direction is licensed-provider orchestration with merchant/provider settlement, no unplanned custody, stored value, money transmission or pooled merchant funds.

That direction still needs a legal classification. For example, official regulators describe authorization regimes for payment-service providers under Kenya’s [National Payment System Act/Regulations](https://www.centralbank.go.ke/national-payments-system/) and Tanzania’s [National Payment Systems Act, 2015](https://www.bot.go.tz/PaymentSystem/regulations?lang=en). These do **not** by themselves prove Ubi is or is not a regulated provider.

Before payment implementation, counsel/provider must document:

- contractual chain and whose account receives customer money;
- whether Ubi transmits instructions, touches credentials/funds, sets settlement or can unilaterally refund;
- merchant onboarding/KYB/KYC/AML/sanctions allocation;
- safeguarding, reconciliation, complaints, reversals/chargebacks and insolvency treatment;
- taxes/fees/currency/FX and receipt responsibility;
- provider licensing and approved country/product/API;
- webhook/payment-data security and incident allocation;
- exit/export/open obligations if provider suspends Ubi/merchant.

No UI copy may say “Ubi holds your deposit” or “paid” unless the approved legal/technical model supports it.

## Electronic acceptance and customer evidence

Country review must decide for each action—Account terms, merchant subscription, booking, quote, cancellation, payment/refund, marketing, privacy consent and call recording:

- required identity/age/authority;
- information displayed before action;
- explicit versus inferred acceptance;
- electronic signature/OTP requirements, if any;
- ability to download/retain terms/receipt;
- corrected/error/revoked acceptance;
- evidence format/retention/admissibility;
- language and accessible presentation;
- cooling-off/cancellation/non-waivable rights;
- merchant/Ubi/provider responsibility.

One “I agree” checkbox cannot lawfully or honestly carry all purposes.

## Privacy implementation checklist per jurisdiction

1. Territorial/material scope and definitions.
2. Ubi legal entity, local establishment/representative and responsible contact.
3. Ubi/merchant/provider controller/processor/joint/independent roles by activity.
4. Registration, renewal, DPO and record/audit filing.
5. Bases/conditions for ordinary, sensitive and child data.
6. Direct versus indirect collection notices and required languages/content.
7. Access/correction/export/erasure/restriction/objection/automated-decision/complaint rights and deadlines.
8. Processor contract, confidentiality, instructions, audit and subprocessor approval.
9. Privacy/security by design, DPIA and prior authorization/consultation.
10. Breach detection, regulator/person/merchant thresholds/deadlines/content.
11. Cross-border transfer/storage/support/remote access and localization.
12. Retention/legal hold/evidence and backup deletion.
13. Marketing/cookies/SDKs/device IDs and communication channel rules.
14. Enforcement, penalties, private claims, appeals and regulator contact.
15. Transition/effective dates and pending regulations/guidance.

## Country selection scorecard

Do not select a country on legal simplicity alone. Score using verified evidence:

- observed merchant/customer pain and willingness to adopt/pay;
- founder/team presence, language and trusted pilot access;
- company/provider/bank/payment setup feasibility;
- Android/network/device realities and notification costs;
- privacy/consumer/payment/telecom obligations and ability to comply;
- local counsel/accounting/security/support availability;
- cloud/provider region, transfer and latency options;
- official WhatsApp/Telegram/Meta/TikTok/payment/SMS eligibility;
- fraud/abuse/safety and dispute operations;
- unit economics and enforceable contracting.

## Required legal advice package

Counsel receives concrete diagrams and prototypes, not a vague “booking app” description:

- legal entities/contracts and money flow;
- actor/responsibility matrix;
- exact first vertical/features and excluded features;
- public/customer/merchant/support/provider journeys;
- data inventory/flow/location/retention/vendors/security;
- message/call/marketing/recording behavior;
- terms/notices/consents/evidence;
- incident/rights/law-enforcement/support processes;
- provider agreements and app permissions.

Written output must state issue, law/source/current date, applicability, owner, required control/document/registration, deadline, residual risk and launch blocker.
